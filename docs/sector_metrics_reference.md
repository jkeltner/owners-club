# Sector metrics reference

The right yardstick depends on how a company makes money: banks are judged on book value and return on equity, software on growth and cash margins, energy producers on free cash flow at a given oil price. This is the full reference a reviewer in wealth management shared, kept here as source material. The main tab's sector lens is the simplified version players will see.

## Equity fundamentals ranked by sector

| Sector | 1 — Most important | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | Use with caution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Banks | ROE and ROTCE | Price-to-tangible book | Net interest margin | Efficiency ratio | Credit quality and CET1 capital | EV/EBITDA; conventional free-cash-flow measures |
| Insurance | ROE and price-to-book | Combined ratio or underwriting margin | Reserve adequacy | Solvency capital | Book-value growth plus dividends | EV/EBITDA; P/E without reserve-cycle analysis |
| Software and SaaS | Revenue growth plus free-cash-flow margin | Net revenue retention | Gross margin | Rule of 40 | EV/Sales or EV/free cash flow | P/E for early-stage firms; EBITDA that adds back heavy stock compensation |
| Semiconductors | Normalized free cash flow | Gross margin | ROIC | Inventory and utilization | Normalized P/E or EV/EBITDA | Peak-cycle P/E; unadjusted PEG |
| Retailers | Same-store sales | Operating margin | Inventory turnover | Free cash flow and ROIC | EV/EBITDAR or P/E | P/S without margin context; EBITDA before lease obligations |
| Consumer staples | Organic sales growth | Gross and operating margins | Free-cash-flow conversion | ROIC | P/E or EV/EBITDA | PEG without assessing brand durability and pricing power |
| Asset-heavy industrials | ROIC versus WACC | Free cash flow after maintenance capex | EV/EBITDA | Incremental ROIC | Backlog and book-to-bill | P/E without cycle normalization; EBITDA without capex |
| Capital-light industrials and services | Organic revenue growth | Operating margin | Free-cash-flow conversion | ROIC | P/E or EV/EBITDA | Book value and P/B |
| Energy exploration and production | Free-cash-flow yield | Net asset value | Breakeven commodity price | Net debt and liquidity | EV to production or reserves | Spot-price P/E and EBITDA without sustaining capex |
| Utilities | Rate-base growth | Funds from operations and dividend coverage | Debt and credit metrics | Allowed versus earned ROE | P/E or EV/EBITDA | Raw free-cash-flow yield, often negative during investment cycles |
| REITs | Adjusted funds from operations per share | Net asset value | Occupancy and same-property NOI growth | Net debt to EBITDA | Dividend coverage | GAAP P/E and EPS |
| Homebuilders | Price-to-tangible book | Normalized ROE | Orders, backlog and cancellations | Land inventory and cash conversion | Normalized P/E | Peak-cycle P/E and quarterly margins in isolation |
| Telecommunications | Free cash flow | Net debt to EBITDA | Subscriber growth and churn | Capital intensity | EV/EBITDA and dividend coverage | P/E without accounting for spectrum and network capex |
| Media and streaming | Free cash flow | Subscriber economics and churn | Content return on investment | Advertising or ARPU trends | EV/EBITDA or DCF | P/E during heavy content-investment periods |
| Pharmaceuticals | Risk-adjusted pipeline value | Patent and revenue concentration | Free cash flow | ROIC on research and acquisitions | P/E or DCF | PEG based on near-term earnings before patent cliffs |
| Biotechnology | Probability-adjusted pipeline value | Cash runway and dilution risk | Clinical and regulatory milestones | Peak-sales potential | Enterprise value relative to pipeline | P/E, EBITDA and book value for pre-revenue firms |
| Payments and fintech | Transaction or payment-volume growth | Take rate and revenue growth | Operating margin and free cash flow | Customer acquisition economics | P/E, PEG or EV/Sales | Gross volume growth without take-rate and credit-loss analysis |
| Asset managers | Assets under management flows | Fee rate and revenue yield | Operating margin | Performance and fund mix | P/E and capital return | P/B unless balance-sheet investments are material |
| Exchanges and data providers | Recurring revenue growth | Operating margin | Free-cash-flow conversion | ROIC | P/E or EV/EBITDA | P/B and near-term volume growth alone |

## Core metrics by business model

| Business model | Primary valuation anchor | Primary quality test | Critical operating indicator | Most common trap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Financial balance sheet | P/TBV, P/B or P/E | ROE or ROTCE adjusted for risk | Credit costs, capital and funding | High ROE created by excess leverage or inadequate reserves |
| Recurring-revenue growth | EV/Sales transitioning to EV/FCF | Retention, gross margin and Rule of 40 | Net revenue retention and customer payback | Ignoring stock compensation, dilution or slowing cohorts |
| Inventory-led retailer | P/E, EV/EBITDAR or DCF | ROIC and cash conversion | Same-store sales and inventory turns | Treating temporary markdowns or working-capital benefits as structural |
| Capital-intensive operator | EV/EBITDA plus DCF | ROIC versus WACC | Utilization, backlog and maintenance capex | Using EBITDA as a proxy for cash flow |
| Commodity producer | NAV and mid-cycle FCF yield | Balance-sheet resilience | Breakeven price and reserve quality | Valuing peak commodity earnings as permanent |
| Regulated or yield-oriented asset | P/E, dividend yield or cash-flow multiple | Coverage and financing capacity | Rate-base growth or contracted cash flow | Ignoring refinancing needs and equity issuance |
| Research-driven company | Risk-adjusted DCF | Pipeline productivity and capital allocation | Milestones, patents and probability of success | Applying current earnings multiples without pipeline context |

## The practical hierarchy

Across sectors, start with the metric that matches the company's economic engine, not the easiest available ratio, then combine valuation, business quality and balance-sheet resilience.

- **Banks:** tangible book value, ROTCE, credit quality and capital matter more than enterprise-value metrics.
- **Software:** growth durability, retention, margins and dilution matter before P/E becomes meaningful.
- **Retailers:** same-store sales and inventory health explain whether margins and cash flow are sustainable.
- **Asset-heavy industrials:** ROIC and free cash flow after maintenance capex are more revealing than EBITDA alone.

The strongest cross-sector framework is normalized free cash flow, incremental ROIC, balance-sheet risk and a reverse DCF. It tests both the company's economics and the expectations already built into its stock price.
